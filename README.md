# VNG Internship Game Prototypes

A collection of **5 game prototypes** developed during my summer internship at **VNG Corporation**.

Each week explored a different game genre and player skill, starting from a specific **design challenge and target player emotion**. I used **Claude to rapidly prototype ideas as HTML games**, then iterated through multiple rounds of mentor feedback to refine the mechanics and player experience.

The projects focus on designing gameplay systems that can consistently evoke specific feelings — from the satisfaction of discovering an unexpected solution to the tension of making a risky decision.

## ⚽ Lets Ball

**Genre:** Casual
**Focus:** Logical Thinking
**Core Emotion:** *"I created a solution nobody else would have thought of."*

### Gameplay

Drag and drop objects from your inventory to guide a falling ball into a cup.

The objects have **simple, familiar rules**, but there are many ways to combine and position them. Players are encouraged to experiment, learn how each object behaves, and eventually discover unconventional combinations that solve the problem.

### Design Challenge

Create an open-ended system where players can use familiar resources in unexpected ways, producing the feeling of **pioneering a solution rather than simply finding the intended answer**.

### Design Approach

The game is built around **simple rules with emergent possibilities**. Instead of teaching players a single correct solution, it gives them enough understanding of the available objects to encourage experimentation.

The goal is to create the kind of discovery found in games like **Minecraft's Redstone or Zelda's systemic puzzles** — where players become familiar with the tools, combine what they have learned, and eventually think of a solution that feels uniquely their own.

**Key Idea:**
*Familiar tools + Open-ended rules → Experimentation → Unexpected combinations → "I can't believe that worked."*

## 🪐 Orbitlink

**Genre:** Hyper-Casual
**Focus:** Timing
**Core Emotion:** *"I was so close. Just one more try."*

### Gameplay

Tap at the right moment to transfer a satellite from one planet to another. Players can take their time to observe the trajectory and carefully choose when to launch.

### Design Challenge

Create a precision challenge where players feel **fully in control of their decision**, making failure feel fair while still creating an intense sense of regret when they miss by a small amount.

### Design Approach

The challenge comes entirely from **timing and precision**. Players are given enough information and time to make a deliberate decision, but the narrow margin for error makes even a small mistake meaningful.

**Key Idea:**
*Control + Precision → Near Miss → Regret → "One more try."*

## 🔷 Shapedrop

**Genre:** Hyper-Casual
**Focus:** Reflex
**Core Emotion:** *Tension without losing control.*

### Gameplay

A shape continuously expands while the player waits for the right moment to tap and stop it. The resulting shape must match the **reference shape displayed directly below**, so the player always knows what they are aiming for.

### Design Challenge

Create a reflex challenge that **starts comfortably**, allowing players to understand the mechanic and build confidence, then gradually introduces enough speed and pressure to create tension — without making the player feel completely out of control.

### Design Approach

The key is balancing **comfort and momentum**.

At first, the player has enough time to understand the relationship between the expanding shape and its target. As the pace increases, the timing window becomes more demanding, forcing the player to react faster while still feeling that success depends on their own ability.

Because the target shape is always visible, the tension comes not from uncertainty about the goal, but from **whether the player can execute the correct timing under increasing pressure**.

**Key Idea:**
*Familiarity → Increasing momentum → Pressure → Reflexive execution → Tension.*

## 🪙 Fool's Gold

**Genre:** Casual
**Focus:** Risk Management
**Core Emotion:** *The thrill of a risky bet paying off.*

### Gameplay

Steal items from different locations with a limited number of turns. Each item has a hidden probability of being genuinely valuable.

Players must decide whether to **play safely or take a risk**, sacrificing turns or inventory items to pursue potentially greater rewards.

### Design Challenge

Create meaningful uncertainty where players must balance **guaranteed rewards against potentially greater but uncertain rewards**, while accepting that even a reasonable decision can sometimes fail because of chance.

### Design Approach

Limited turns and hidden information force players to constantly evaluate the value of risk. Players can protect what they have, spend resources to pursue better opportunities, or gamble on an uncertain outcome.

**Key Idea:**
*Hidden information + Limited resources → Risk calculation → High-risk decision → High-reward satisfaction.*

## ⚓ Naval Combat

**Genre:** Tower Defense
**Focus:** Planning
**Core Emotion:** *"I saw that coming."*

### Gameplay

Players control enemy movement by manipulating **Threat** rather than simply attacking enemies directly.

* **Threat Boats** redirect normal enemy boats.
* Upgraded Threat can influence stronger **Tankers**.
* **Jammers** can ignore Threat and continue straight toward their destination.
* Different enemy and player units counter one another, requiring careful calculation before committing resources.

### Design Challenge

Create a Tower Defense system where information is **clear and predictable**, allowing players to think several steps ahead and build long-term plans with multiple viable solutions.

### Design Approach

Enemy movement becomes part of the player's strategic toolkit. By understanding how each enemy responds to different types of Threat, players can predict future paths, arrange their defenses, and intentionally create favorable situations.

**Key Idea:**
*Transparent information + Predictable interactions → Prediction → Long-term planning → "I knew this would happen."*

## 🎯 Design Philosophy

Across these prototypes, I explored how **game mechanics can be designed around a specific target emotion** rather than simply providing a set of mechanics.

| Prototype        | Target Experience                                                     |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Lets Ball**    | The satisfaction of discovering a solution nobody else would think of |
| **Orbitlink**    | The regret of a near miss and the urge to try again                   |
| **Shapedrop**    | Building confidence before pushing the player into controlled tension |
| **Fool's Gold**  | The excitement of taking a risk and getting rewarded                  |
| **Naval Combat** | The satisfaction of predicting an outcome several moves ahead         |

The central design question throughout the internship was:

> **How can gameplay systems create a specific emotion, even when players approach the game in different ways?**
